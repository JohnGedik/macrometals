Silver carries two histories at once. It is a monetary metal, but it is also an industrial input whose demand depends on factories, grids, and electronics.

That combination can make silver lag gold during defensive episodes and catch up when industrial expectations improve. The gold-to-silver ratio is one way to frame that changing balance.

Ratios are context, not forecasts. Their value is in helping investors notice when the market's emphasis is shifting from monetary protection to cyclical growth, or back again.
